● Chapter 05

5 Mistakes That Will Ruin Your First Job

  1. Skipping Surface Prep This is the #1 killer. If you don't grind and profile the concrete properly, your epoxy will peel up in weeks. No amount of beautiful metallic work saves a bad prep job. Grind it, vacuum it, grind it again.
  2. Not Checking Moisture Levels Concrete holds moisture. If the slab is too wet, the epoxy won't bond. Use a simple moisture test (tape a plastic sheet down for 24 hours) or invest in a moisture meter. One $20 test saves you a $3,000 redo.
  3. Mixing The Wrong Ratios Epoxy is chemistry. If you eyeball the Part A / Part B ratio instead of measuring it, you'll get a soft, sticky mess that never fully cures. Follow the product spec sheet to the letter.
  4. Working In Extreme Temperatures Epoxy doesn't cure properly in freezing cold or extreme heat. Ideal application range is 55°F – 85°F. If it's 100°F in a garage with no AC, you have about 10 minutes before your product starts kicking too fast.
  5. Undercharging Your First Jobs Everyone does this. You're scared to charge "too much" so you bid $800 on a job that should be $3,000. You burn yourself out, hate the work, and quit. Price for profit from Day 1. This isn't a hobby — it's a business.
